Abstract
   Vitamin D signaling exerts pleiotropic biological effects through activation of the vitamin D receptor (VDR), where transcriptional responses to vitamin D and its analogs show substantial variability across cellular contexts. To systematically characterize these responses, we analyzed publicly available LINCS L1000 perturbational transcriptomic data comprising 258 signatures across five human cell lines and seven vitamin D–related compounds.
   Global transcriptomic analysis indicated that cellular context contributes slightly more to transcriptional variability than compound identity and dose. At the gene level, we identified a limited set of recurrently modulated genes and defined a consensus transcriptional core (42 upregulated and 35 downregulated genes), which was used to derive a composite transcriptional activity metric (core_score). Despite strong context-dependent variability at the gene level, pathway-level analysis showed recurrent patterns toward shared functional programs, including metabolic regulation, stress response, and proliferative control.
   Dose–response analysis revealed heterogeneous transcriptional sensitivity in a dependent manner of cell lines, with monotonic relationships. Importantly, variability in transcriptional responses was not obviously associated with differential modulation of the VDR transcriptional axis, as VDR transcript levels remained largely stable across conditions, supporting a model in which vitamin D signaling operates through activation of pre-existing receptor pools.
   Together, these findings are consistent with hierarchical organization of vitamin D–induced transcriptional regulation, in which conserved downstream biological programs emerge from context-dependent gene-level responses. This study provides a reproducible framework for integrating large-scale perturbational datasets to disentangle shared and context-specific transcriptional programs.

Highlights

1. Introduction
   Vitamin D is a secosteroid hormone that plays a central role in calcium homeostasis, skeletal integrity and in a wide range of non-classical biological processes, including immune regulation, cellular proliferation, differentiation and metabolic control [1,2]. These pleiotropic effects are primarily mediated through the vitamin D receptor (VDR), a ligand-activated receptor that regulates gene expression by binding to vitamin D response elements (VDREs) in target genes in association with retinoid X receptor (RXR) and other co-regulatory factors [3].
   Despite a well-established molecular framework for VDR signaling, transcriptional responses to vitamin D and its synthetic analogs exhibit substantial variability across biological systems. This variability has been attributed to differences in chromatin accessibility, co-regulator availability, baseline transcriptional states, and ligand metabolism across cell types, leading to context-dependent gene regulation [3,4]. Mostly, vitamin D signaling is better understood as a modulatory system whose downstream effects are shaped by cellular context rather than dictated solely by receptor activation. The context-dependent nature of vitamin D signaling has important implications for therapeutic applications. Differential transcriptional responses across tissues may underlie variability in clinical outcomes and influence the design of vitamin D analogs with tissue-selective activity, aiming to maximize beneficial effects (e.g., in bone or immune regulation) while minimizing adverse outcomes such as hypercalcemia [1,3].
   However, most transcriptomic studies of vitamin D signaling have been conducted in specific cellular models or focused on individual compounds, limiting the ability to identify transcriptional programs that are consistently engaged across diverse biological contexts. While these studies have provided important insights into VDR-mediated gene regulation, they often lack a systematic, cross-context framework that allows disentangling shared transcriptional responses from cell type–specific effects. In addition, variability in experimental design, including differences in dose, exposure time, and analytical approaches, further complicates direct comparison across studies.
   Large-scale perturbational resources such as the LINCS L1000 dataset provide an opportunity to address these limitations by enabling systematic analysis of transcriptional responses across multiple compounds, doses and cellular contexts within a unified experimental and computational framework [5]. By leveraging such datasets, it becomes possible to characterize both conserved and context-dependent transcriptional programs associated with vitamin D signaling in a reproducible and scalable manner.
   The objective of this study was to perform a systematic transcriptomic characterization of vitamin D–related perturbations using publicly available LINCS L1000 data, with the aim of identifying recurrent gene expression patterns and conserved functional programs across diverse cellular contexts. By integrating perturbational signatures across multiple compounds, doses, and cell lines within a unified analytical framework, this study seeks to quantify the relative contributions of shared and context-dependent transcriptional responses. In addition, we define a reproducible transcriptional core based on consensus gene-level effects and introduce a composite metric (core_score) to capture coordinated transcriptional activity across signatures. Rather than focusing on individual genes or specific experimental systems, this approach provides a system-level perspective on vitamin D signaling, enabling the identification of robust transcriptional patterns that are consistent across heterogeneous biological contexts.
2. Materials and Methods
2.1 Dataset selection and preprocessing
   Publicly available transcriptomic profiles were obtained from the LINCS L1000 perturbational dataset (Level 5 MODZ consensus signatures), together with associated metadata and compound annotation files.
   Vitamin D–related perturbations were initially identified through a broad keyword-based screening of compound annotation fields, including terms such as “vitamin D”, “VDR”, “calcitriol”, and “calciferol”. This screening step was designed to capture a comprehensive set of candidate compounds potentially associated with vitamin D signaling.
   Candidate compounds were subsequently curated based on annotation relevance and experimental coverage. Selection criteria prioritized compounds with: (i) reported association with vitamin D receptor signaling, (ii) availability of replicate-supported signatures, and (iii) sufficient representation across multiple cell lines and dose levels under comparable experimental conditions. To ensure consistency across perturbations, only signatures corresponding to a treatment duration of 24 hours were retained.
   Based on these criteria, a final set of seven vitamin D agonists and analogs was selected for downstream analysis: calcitriol, calcipotriol, maxacalcitol, seocalcitol, ercalcitriol, tacalcitol, and paricalcitol. Compounds with ambiguous annotation, limited representation or insufficient cross-context coverage were excluded from the final analytical set.
   To ensure robustness of gene-level estimates, only signatures supported by at least three biological replicates were included. Replicate support was determined based on metadata annotations provided in the LINCS dataset. Signatures failing to meet this criterion were excluded.
Cell lines were retained for downstream analysis if they met the following criteria: representation across multiple vitamin D–related compounds, availability of multiple dose levels and sufficient replicate-supported signatures to permit cross-context comparisons. Application of these criteria resulted in inclusion of five human cell lines (A549, HA1E, MCF7, PC3, and U2OS).
   Dose values were extracted from LINCS metadata and treated as continuous variables in downstream analyses. When multiple doses were available for a given compound–cell line combination, each dose-specific signature was retained independently to enable dose–response modeling.
   All gene-level analyses were performed using the full L1000 gene space (landmark and inferred genes), as provided in Level 5 signatures. No additional batch correction was applied beyond the consensus normalization implemented by the LINCS consortium.
   Metadata were linked to gene expression profiles via unique signature identifiers, ensuring direct mapping between perturbational annotations and transcriptional signatures.
   The resulting curated dataset comprised 258 replicate-supported perturbation signatures corresponding to seven vitamin D–related compounds across five human cell lines, all measured at 24 hours post-treatment.
   All downstream analyses were performed at the signature level unless otherwise specified. Analyses were conducted using Python-based data analysis workflows.
   
2.2 Principal component analysis and variance partitioning
   Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to characterize the global structure of transcriptional variation across vitamin D–related perturbational signatures. Analyses were based on the LINCS Level 5 gene expression matrix including both landmark and inferred genes after exclusion of signatures containing missing values. The resulting matrix was transposed to a signature-by-gene format, such that rows corresponded to perturbational signatures and columns to genes. Before PCA, gene features were standardized across signatures by mean-centering and scaling to unit variance, a common preprocessing step for multivariate analyses of high-dimensional gene expression data [6].
   PCA is widely used in transcriptomic studies to summarize dominant axes of variation across samples and to identify major sources of transcriptional heterogeneity [7]. This analysis was computed on the standardized signature-level expression matrix, and the first two principal components were retained for visualization of global transcriptional structure.
   To quantify the contribution of experimental factors to global transcriptional variability, a Euclidean distance matrix was calculated from the standardized gene expression matrix. This distance matrix was used for permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA), which assesses the effect of explanatory variables on multivariate datasets using permutation-based significance testing [8]. The analysis evaluated the effects of cell line identity, compound identity and perturbation dose on the multivariate transcriptional space. PERMANOVA was implemented in R using the vegan package [9] with 999 permutations and marginal tests for each term.
   
2.3 Gene-level transcriptional analysis
   To characterize gene-level transcriptional responses associated with vitamin D–related perturbations, genes were ranked according to their mean absolute z-score across all perturbational signatures. This ranking strategy prioritizes genes showing both strong and consistent transcriptional modulation across the dataset while remaining agnostic to the direction of regulation, an approach commonly used to summarize differential transcriptional responses in large-scale perturbational datasets [5].
   Gene-level variance across perturbational signatures was calculated on the expression matrix to identify genes exhibiting the greatest transcriptional variability across the dataset. Highly variable genes were selected to capture dominant patterns of transcriptional heterogeneity in the data [10].
   For visualization of transcriptional co-regulation patterns, the 20 genes with the highest variance across signatures were selected, and hierarchical clustering was performed on the resulting gene-by-signature matrix using Euclidean distance and complete linkage. Heatmap columns were annotated according to cell line identity using signature metadata. 
   Hierarchical clustering is widely used to explore gene expression structure and identify co-regulated transcriptional programs in high-dimensional transcriptomic datasets [11].
   
2.4 Identification of consensus vitamin D–responsive gene sets and core_score computation
   To identify transcriptional responses consistently associated with vitamin D perturbations across cellular contexts, a consensus-based gene selection strategy was applied using a vote-count aggregation approach on gene-level transcriptional effects.
   Gene-level effects were first summarized within each cell line by averaging LINCS Level 5 moderated z-scores across all perturbational signatures corresponding to that cell line. This produced a gene-by-cell-line matrix of mean transcriptional effects, where each value represented the average response of a gene within a given cellular context.
   Within each cell line, genes were ranked independently according to their mean transcriptional effect. To capture the most strongly modulated genes while reducing noise from intermediate values, the extremes of the ranked distribution were selected. Specifically, for each cell line, the 50 genes with the largest positive effects and the 50 genes with the largest negative effects were retained.
   To identify genes exhibiting recurrent transcriptional responses across cellular contexts, a vote-count procedure was applied. For each gene, the number of cell lines in which it appeared among the selected extremes was counted separately for the upregulated and downregulated directions.
   Consensus gene sets were defined using a minimum recurrence criterion. Genes present in at least two cell lines were selected as consensus genes. This criterion yielded two consensus gene sets representing recurrent transcriptional responses to vitamin D perturbations: 42 upregulated genes and 35 downregulated genes.
   These consensus gene sets were used to define a composite transcriptional response metric (core_score) for each perturbational signature. For each signature, the core_score was computed as the difference between the mean expression of the upregulated consensus genes and the mean expression of the downregulated consensus genes, using the moderated z-scores provided by LINCS Level 5 data.
   The core_score was designed to capture coordinated transcriptional shifts across a consensus set of consistently modulated genes, rather than to model pharmacological dose–response relationships at the level of individual genes or compounds. By summarizing the average behavior of recurrently modulated genes across cellular contexts, this approach provides a compact representation of shared transcriptional responses while preserving directionality (up- vs down-regulation). Sensitivity analyses were performed using alternative gene-selection thresholds (top 30 and top 100 per direction) to evaluate the robustness of the core_score definition.
   
2.5 Pathway enrichment analysis
   To investigate functional programs associated with vitamin D–related transcriptional responses, pathway-level enrichment analysis was performed using the Hallmark gene set collection from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [12]. Gene sets were loaded from symbol-based GMT files, and gene sets containing fewer than 15 or more than 500 genes were excluded prior to testing.
   Enrichment analysis was conducted using a preranked GSEA-like framework implemented in Python. Gene-level input consisted of the effects_by_cell matrix, in which rows corresponded to genes and columns to cell lines, and values represented gene-level transcriptional effects calculated as mean LINCS Level 5 z-scores averaged across signatures within each cell line. For each cell line, genes were ranked according to these mean z-scores to generate preranked gene lists, yielding 12,327 ranked genes per cell line. Gene identifiers were mapped to gene symbols prior to enrichment analysis, and when multiple entries were mapped to the same symbol, duplicate entries were resolved by retaining the entry with the largest absolute score.
   For each gene set, an enrichment score (ES) was computed using a running-sum statistic analogous to the original GSEA approach [13]. Statistical significance was assessed using permutation-based null distributions generated from 400 permutations of the preranked gene list. Empirical p-values were calculated from the permutation results and adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure [14]. Pathways with FDR < 0.05 were considered significant.
   Enrichment analyses were performed independently for each cell line. For visualization, the top 10 significant pathways per cell line were retained after ranking by adjusted significance and enrichment score. In the dot plot summary, pathway significance was represented as ?log10(FDR), while point size corresponded to gene set size.
   To identify recurrent pathway-level responses across cell lines, significant pathways (FDR < 0.05) were aggregated and summarized by the number of cell lines in which each pathway was significantly enriched (n_cells). For each pathway, a mean enrichment score (mean_ES) was calculated across the subset of cell lines in which that pathway reached statistical significance. Pathways were ranked by n_cells and mean_ES, and the top 10 Hallmark pathways were retained for consensus-level visualization.
   
2.6 Dose–response analysis.
   Dose values were obtained from LINCS metadata and log10-transformed prior to analysis, a common transformation used in pharmacological dose–response modeling to linearize relationships between ligand concentration and biological response [15]. The resulting dataset consisted of paired observations of transcriptional response (core_score) and ligand concentration for each perturbational signature.
   Dose–response relationships were evaluated separately within each cell line. Monotonic associations between dose and transcriptional response were assessed using Spearman rank correlation [16]. To quantify uncertainty, 95% confidence intervals for Spearman correlation coefficients were estimated using bootstrap resampling (4,000 iterations) of the paired observations within each cell line.
   To quantify dose–response sensitivity, linear regression models were fitted using log10-transformed dose as predictor and core_score as response variable (core_score ~ log10(dose)). Models were estimated using ordinary least squares with heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC3) robust standard errors, which provide improved inference in the presence of heteroskedasticity and moderate sample sizes [17]. Regression coefficients and their 95% confidence intervals were computed using the Wald approximation based on robust standard errors.
   Given that the analysis aggregates multiple compounds with heterogeneous dose coverage, the resulting dose–response relationships should be interpreted as dataset-level trends rather than intrinsic pharmacological dose–response curves for individual compounds. Distributions of dosses across vitamin D analogs are shown in Supplementary Fig. S2.
   
2.7 Identification of shared and cell line–specific transcriptional responses
   Genes were considered transcriptionally modulated within each cell line based on the magnitude of their aggregated response across perturbational signatures. Specifically, gene-level effects were first summarized by computing the mean LINCS Level 5 moderated z-score for each gene within each cell line. Modulation thresholds were then defined using the distribution of gene-level mean absolute z-scores within each cell line. Genes whose absolute mean z-score exceeded the 90th percentile of this distribution were classified as modulated.
   To assess the extent of shared and cell line–specific responses, modulated genes were compared across cell lines. Genes detected as modulated in all five cell lines were defined as shared, whereas genes not consistently detected across all cell lines were classified as cell line specific. This definition captures both uniquely modulated genes and those shared across a subset of cellular contexts. Intersections among gene sets were visualized using UpSet plots, which provide a scalable alternative to traditional Venn diagrams for representing overlaps across multiple sets [18]. To further characterize these groups, the distribution of mean absolute z-scores was compared between shared and cell line–specific genes, allowing evaluation of whether recurrently modulated genes exhibit stronger transcriptional effects than context-specific responses.
   
2.8 Functional annotation of the shared gene core
   Hallmark pathway enrichment patterns across cell lines were summarized using a heatmap of pathway-level enrichment scores. Pathways were selected using a reproducible ranking strategy based on (i) presence across cell lines and (ii) average enrichment score across cell lines. Specifically, Hallmark pathways detected in at least four cell lines were ranked by the number of cell lines in which they were present and subsequently by their mean enrichment score across cell lines, and the top 10 pathways were retained for visualization. Heatmap cells display the enrichment score (ES) for each pathway–cell line pair.
   Functional characterization of the shared vitamin D–responsive gene core was performed using the Hallmark gene set collection from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB v2025.1, Human; file: h.all.v2025.1.Hs.symbols.gmt) [12]. Gene symbols belonging to the shared transcriptional core were mapped to Hallmark gene sets to identify pathways containing each gene.
   For each gene in the shared set, all Hallmark pathways in which the gene was annotated were recorded. Because the shared core consisted of a limited number of genes, functional interpretation was based on direct pathway membership rather than additional enrichment testing. This approach enables identification of biological programs recurrently represented among conserved vitamin D–responsive genes while avoiding statistical instability associated with enrichment testing on small gene sets.
2.9 Analysis of the VDR transcriptional axis
   To examine whether differences in downstream transcriptional responses across cell lines were associated with modulation of the vitamin D receptor signaling axis, gene-level transcriptional responses were evaluated for key components of the canonical pathway, including VDR, CYP24A1, CYP27B1, and RXRA.
   For each gene and cell line, transcriptional responses were summarized as the mean LINCS Level 5 moderated z-score across all perturbational signatures belonging to that cell line. These values represent average transcript-level modulation following vitamin D–related perturbations.
   The resulting gene-by-cell-line matrix was visualized using a heatmap to compare the magnitude and direction of transcriptional responses across cellular contexts.
   
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Dataset curation and definition of vitamin D perturbation signatures
   Publicly available LINCS L1000 perturbational transcriptomic data were filtered according to predefined inclusion criteria (see Methods) to generate a focused dataset of vitamin D–related transcriptional signatures suitable for cross-context comparative analysis. To ensure uniformity of experimental conditions, all analyses were restricted to perturbations measured at 24 hours post-treatment. This time point was selected both for consistency and because it represents the most abundantly sampled exposure condition in the dataset, whereas other time points were comparatively underrepresented. Vitamin D–related perturbation signatures were retained if they corresponded to one of seven predefined compounds and were supported by at least three biological replicates. Only cell lines with sufficient representation across compounds and dose levels were included in downstream analyses. Following application of these criteria, the final curated dataset comprised 258 perturbation signatures across five human cell lines and seven vitamin D–related compounds, all measured at a standardized 24-hour time point. These signatures were derived from LINCS Level 5 consensus (MODZ) profiles and linked to corresponding metadata using unique signature identifiers. This curated dataset served as the foundation for all subsequent gene-level, pathway-level, and cross-context comparative analyses.
3.2 Global structure of vitamin D–induced transcriptomic responses
   To characterize the overall structure of transcriptomic responses induced by vitamin D–related perturbations, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the curated set of perturbation signatures. This dimensionality reduction enabled visualization of the main axes of variation across compounds and cellular contexts.
   Projection of signatures onto the first two principal components revealed a structured distribution of samples (Figure 1A–B). When colored by compound, vitamin D–related perturbations exhibited substantial overlap in PCA space, indicating broadly shared transcriptional effects across different analogs (Figure 1A). However, signatures did not collapse into a single cluster, suggesting heterogeneity in the magnitude and direction of transcriptional responses.
   In contrast, visualization of the same embedding colored by cell line revealed more distinct grouping patterns driven by cellular context (Figure 1B). Signatures corresponding to different cell lines occupied partially distinct regions of PCA space, indicating that cell line identity contributes to transcriptional variability in response to vitamin D perturbations.
   The scree plot (Figure 1C) showed that variance is distributed across multiple principal components. Although the first components capture a substantial fraction of the total variance, no single component alone is sufficient to describe the overall structure, consistent with a multidimensional transcriptional response landscape.
   To formally quantify the contribution of experimental factors to transcriptional variability, we performed permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA) on the full signature-level expression space. Both cell line identity (R² = 0.072, F = 5.04, p = 0.001) and compound identity (R² = 0.058, F = 1.48, p = 0.001) contributed significantly to global variance, with cellular context explaining a slightly larger fraction of the variability. Perturbation dose showed a smaller but statistically significant effect (R² = 0.013, F = 3.57, p = 0.001), indicating that dose contributes modestly to the global organization of the dataset. These R² values reflect marginal effects and should be interpreted as relative contributions rather than additive or mutually exclusive sources of variance.

Each point represents an individual transcriptional signature, and colors denote different vitamin D compounds and analogs. (B) PCA of the same transcriptional signatures, colored by cell line. (C) Scree plot showing the proportion of variance explained by individual principal components (blue) and the cumulative explained variance (orange). 

   The overlap observed across vitamin D analogs is consistent with previous studies showing that structurally distinct compounds can engage the vitamin D receptor (VDR) and elicit broadly convergent biological effects despite differences in ligand structure and transcriptional signatures [3,19]. In contrast, the separation across cell lines reflects the influence of lineage-specific regulatory environments, including chromatin accessibility and co-regulatory factor availability, which have been shown to shape VDR-dependent transcriptional responses in a cell-type–specific manner [4].
   These results indicate that cell line identity contributes slightly more to transcriptional variability than compound identity and dose among the factors evaluated. This pattern suggests that the transcriptional effects of vitamin D are primarily shaped by pre-existing regulatory states rather than by ligand concentration alone.
3.3 Gene-level transcriptional responses to vitamin D analog exposure
   To characterize gene-level transcriptional programs associated with vitamin D analog perturbations, we examined genes showing the strongest and most consistent modulation across all signatures. Genes were ranked based on their mean absolute z-score across perturbations, capturing the magnitude of transcriptional responses across compounds and cellular contexts.
   Figure 2A summarizes the top 20 most modulated genes identified by this criterion. Several genes exhibited recurrent regulation across signatures, including IGFBP3, DDIT4, TSKU, TXNRD1, and NFKBIA, highlighting pathways related to growth factor signaling, cellular stress responses, redox regulation, and inflammatory signaling. The presence of these genes among the most strongly modulated ones indicates that vitamin D analog exposure is associated with recurrent transcriptional responses rather than purely signature-specific effects.
   To investigate the structure of transcriptional variability at the gene level, we performed hierarchical clustering of the most variable genes across all perturbation signatures (Figure 2B). Gene variability was defined based on the variance of z-scored expression across signatures, selecting genes that capture major patterns of transcriptional heterogeneity. Clustering based on z-scored expression values revealed organized patterns of gene co-regulation, with signatures grouping according to shared transcriptional profiles. These patterns were largely aligned with cellular context, as indicated by the enrichment of specific cell lines within clusters, suggesting that cell identity contributes to gene-level transcriptional organization.
   Importantly, these rankings reflect distinct criteria: mean absolute z-score captures the magnitude of transcriptional modulation, whereas variance reflects heterogeneity of gene responses across signatures.

(A) Top 20 most modulated genes ranked by mean absolute z-score across all perturbation signatures, reflecting the magnitude of transcriptional responses.    Together, these analyses indicate that vitamin D analog perturbations induce recurrent modulation of a subset of genes, while the broader structure of transcriptional variability is strongly influenced by cell line–specific contexts. This gene-level organization provides a foundation for subsequent functional and pathway-level analyses.
   The recurrent modulation of genes such as IGFBP3, DDIT4, TXNRD1, and NFKBIA across vitamin D analog perturbations is consistent with previous reports identifying these genes as responsive components of vitamin D–regulated transcriptional networks. IGFBP3 has been widely implicated as a vitamin D–responsive gene involved in growth factor signaling and cell fate regulation [20], while DDIT4 and TXNRD1 link vitamin D signaling to cellular stress adaptation and redox homeostasis [21,22]. Similarly, the regulation of NFKBIA supports established crosstalk between vitamin D signaling and inflammatory pathways mediated by NF-?B [23]. Importantly, although these genes emerge as recurrent targets across perturbations, their coordinated expression patterns remain strongly shaped by cellular context, in agreement with prior transcriptomic and epigenomic studies demonstrating that VDR-mediated gene regulation is highly dependent on cell-type–specific chromatin landscapes and co-regulatory environments [3,24]. Together, these observations support a model in which vitamin D analogs elicit recurrent gene-level responses, while the broader transcriptional coordination varies in magnitude and structure across cellular backgrounds.
3.4 Pathway-level characterization of vitamin D perturbations
   To investigate the functional programs underlying vitamin D–induced transcriptional responses, we performed pathway-level enrichment analysis using Hallmark gene sets applied to LINCS-derived transcriptional signatures.
   Enrichment analysis at the cell line level revealed a consistent set of pathways associated with vitamin D exposure (Figure 3A), including TGF-? signaling, UV response, xenobiotic metabolism, apical junction, KRAS signaling, reactive oxygen species pathway, hypoxia, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, TNF-? signaling via NF-?B, and myogenesis. Although enrichment magnitude varied across cell lines, the overall pathway repertoire was largely conserved, indicating engagement of common functional programs across diverse cellular contexts.
   Consensus enrichment analysis further identified pathways recurrently enriched across multiple cell lines (Figure 3B), including unfolded protein response, IL2–STAT5 signaling, adipogenesis, xenobiotic metabolism, glycolysis, estrogen response (early), KRAS signaling (up), and UV response. These pathways point to coordinated modulation of stress response, metabolic regulation, and signaling processes in response to vitamin D perturbations [25–27].
   To relate pathway-level enrichment patterns to underlying gene-level responses, a heatmap of effect sizes across cell lines for the top-ranked genes based on mean absolute z-score is shown in Figure 3C. Genes such as IGFBP3, DDIT4, TXNRD1, NFKBIA, and TSKU were consistently modulated across multiple cell lines, supporting their role as recurrently modulated genes across cellular contexts.

   Despite these overall recurrent patterns, pathway engagement was not uniform across all models. In particular, MCF7 cells did not exhibit enrichment of the recurrent Hallmarks identified in other cell lines among their top-ranked pathways, indicating a distinct transcriptional response profile in this context [28]. This variability is consistent with previous studies showing heterogeneous vitamin D–induced gene expression across cell types [29,30] and highlights the influence of cellular context in shaping pathway-level responses. Notably, this distinct behavior may reflect the hormone-responsive transcriptional program of MCF7 cells, suggesting a potential interaction between vitamin D signaling and estrogen receptor–mediated regulation, as previously reported in hormone-responsive systems [31].
3.5 Dose-dependent transcriptional sensitivity to vitamin D varies across cell lines
   To assess whether transcriptional responses to vitamin D follow a graded, dose-dependent pattern, we evaluated the relationship between dose and the consensus transcriptional response (core_score; see Section 2.4) across cell lines: MCF7 (n = 70), A549 (n = 56), PC3 (n = 57), U2OS (n = 21), and HA1E (n = 54).
   Monotonic associations between dose and core_score were observed in MCF7, A549, and PC3 cells, with moderate positive Spearman correlations (MCF7, ? = 0.632, 95% CI 0.459–0.767; A549, ? = 0.570, 95% CI 0.313–0.771; PC3, ? = 0.562, 95% CI 0.339–0.743). U2OS cells showed a weaker but still positive association (? = 0.487, 95% CI 0.052–0.785), whereas HA1E cells displayed minimal evidence of a dose-ordered response (? = 0.179, 95% CI ?0.098 to 0.445).
   Linear regression models (core_score ~ log10(dose)) further supported these patterns (Figure 4), revealing positive dose–response slopes across all cell lines, with statistically significant effects in MCF7, A549, PC3, and U2OS, but not in HA1E. Together, these results indicate that monotonicity and effect size capture complementary aspects of dose-dependent transcriptional behavior.
   To assess the robustness of the core_score definition, we repeated the analysis using alternative gene-selection thresholds (top 30 and top 100 genes per direction per cell line). Core scores derived from these alternative definitions showed strong positive correlations with the original top-50 score across perturbational signatures (Spearman ? = 0.80 for top30 vs top50 and ? = 0.87 for top100 vs top50), ...indicating that the score is not strongly dependent on the specific cutoff used to define the consensus gene sets (Supplementary Fig. S3).
   To further characterize these trends, we performed additional analyses stratifying dose–response models by individual vitamin D analogs (Supplementary Figure S1). Across compounds, dose–response slopes were consistently positive, with several analogs (including ercalcitriol, seocalcitol, tacalcitol, and calcitriol) showing statistically significant associations. Although effect sizes varied, no analog exhibited an inverse trend, supporting that the aggregate analysis reflects a general pattern of transcriptional modulation rather than being driven by a specific compound. Consistent with these findings, the distribution of core_score values across compounds showed comparable ranges and substantial within-compound variability (Supplementary Fig. S4), supporting that the observed transcriptional response is not driven by a single analog.

   Dose–response analysis further suggests that transcriptional engagement by vitamin D is not only context-dependent in structure but also in quantitative sensitivity. The presence of monotonic associations between dose and core transcriptional response in several cell lines is compatible with general features of nuclear receptor pharmacodynamics, in which ligand concentration modulates transcriptional activation in a graded manner [32]. Previous studies have similarly shown that activation of the vitamin D receptor by agonists can modulate target gene expression in proportion to ligand stimulation [33]. In contrast, the absence of a detectable monotonic relationship in HA1E cells suggests reduced transcriptional responsiveness or altered regulatory coupling between ligand exposure and downstream transcriptional output. Such differences may reflect variation in receptor abundance, co-regulator availability, chromatin accessibility, or signal buffering capacity across cellular contexts [34]. Collectively, these observations support the idea that cellular identity influences not only the structure of vitamin D–induced transcriptional programs but also their quantitative responsiveness to ligand concentration. Because the LINCS dataset includes multiple compounds and heterogeneous sampling across dose levels, the observed monotonic relationships should be interpreted as dataset-level dose–response trends rather than strict pharmacological curves for individual compounds.
3.6 Cell line–specific versus shared transcriptomic responses
   To characterize conserved and context-dependent transcriptional effects of vitamin D–related perturbations, we compared gene- and pathway-level responses across five cell lines (A549, HA1E, MCF7, PC3, and U2OS). Genes were defined as transcriptionally modulated within each cell line using a threshold corresponding to the 90th percentile of gene-level mean absolute z-scores. This definition is distinct from the consensus gene sets used to compute the core_score (Section 2.4), which capture recurrence across at least two cell lines.
   UpSet analysis showed that most modulated genes were cell line–specific, indicating strong context dependence (Figure 5A). In contrast, a small subset of genes (n = 19) was consistently modulated across all five cell lines, defining a shared transcriptional core (Figure 5A).
   Despite its limited size, this shared gene set exhibited distinct quantitative properties. Genes in the shared core displayed higher mean absolute z-scores than cell line–specific genes (Figure 5B), whereas cell line–specific responses were characterized by a larger number of genes with smaller effect sizes. This pattern indicates that shared genes represent consistently stronger transcriptional responses rather than threshold-dependent artifacts.
   At the pathway level, recurrent enrichment patterns were observed across cell lines. Several Hallmark pathways, including estrogen response, hypoxia, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and xenobiotic metabolism, were enriched in multiple cell lines, although with variable magnitude (Figure 5C). PC3 cells showed consistently higher enrichment scores across pathways, whereas U2OS cells exhibited comparatively weaker responses, indicating quantitative rather than qualitative differences in pathway engagement.

   Together, these findings support a hierarchical model of vitamin D–induced transcriptional regulation, in which a conserved transcriptional core coexists with extensive context-dependent variability across cellular backgrounds, consistent with previous transcriptomic studies showing heterogeneous vitamin D–induced gene expression across cell types [38].
3.7 Functional characterization of the shared vitamin D–responsive gene core
   To further characterize the transcriptional core shared across all five cell lines, we examined the membership of the 19 shared genes in the MSigDB Hallmark gene set collection (Table 1). Importantly, genes identified under the stricter threshold were fully contained within the original consensus definition, supporting the robustness of the identified transcriptional core.
   Mapping of these genes revealed a functional composition dominated by pathways associated with cellular stress adaptation, metabolic regulation, and proteostatic control. The most recurrent categories included mTORC1 signaling and unfolded protein response, together with pathways linked to cell cycle regulation and genome integrity, such as E2F targets, p53 signaling, and DNA repair. In addition, components of inflammatory and cytokine-associated signaling, including TNF-? signaling via NF-?B and interferon gamma response, were represented within the shared gene set.
   Metabolic pathways beyond mTORC1 signaling—including glycolysis, lipid metabolism, and xenobiotic metabolism—were also present, although with lower representation, while a smaller subset of genes was associated with context-dependent programs such as epithelial–mesenchymal transition, angiogenesis, and hormone response pathways (Table 1).

   The identification of this shared transcriptional core further supports the existence of conserved vitamin D–responsive programs despite strong context-dependent variability across cellular systems. 3.8 Limited modulation of the VDR transcriptional axis across cell lines
   To determine whether variability in downstream transcriptional responses could be explained by modulation of the VDR transcriptional axis, we examined expression changes of VDR and key components of its canonical pathway—CYP24A1, CYP27B1, and RXRA—across all five cell lines (Figure 6).
   As shown in Figure 6, transcript-level changes in VDR were consistently small across cell lines, with no evidence of transcriptional induction following vitamin D–related perturbations, although U2OS displayed the largest negative deviation. CYP27B1 likewise remained close to baseline across all contexts. In contrast, CYP24A1 exhibited modest, context-dependent positive modulation, with detectable increases in A549 and stronger induction in HA1E, while remaining near baseline in MCF7, PC3, and U2OS. RXRA showed modest but directionally consistent positive modulation in a subset of cell lines, most notably in MCF7 and U2OS.

   These patterns indicate that heterogeneity in vitamin D–responsive transcriptional programs is unlikely to be primarily driven by differences in VDR transcript abundance across cell lines. Instead, vitamin D signaling appears to operate largely through activation of pre-existing receptor pools, with downstream transcriptional outcomes shaped by cellular context, including chromatin accessibility, co-regulator composition, and baseline transcriptional state [1,2]. Similarly, CYP27B1 showed minimal variation, while CYP24A1 displayed context-dependent induction consistent with its role as a ligand-activated VDR target involved in feedback regulation of vitamin D metabolism [43–45].
   Together, these findings indicate that variability in transcriptional responses is not primarily driven by differences in VDR transcript abundance, but rather than by downstream regulatory mechanisms. Previous studies have shown that such variability is largely governed by chromatin accessibility, co-regulator composition, ligand metabolism, and baseline transcriptional state [3,46]. Consistent with this framework, vitamin D signaling operates predominantly through activation of pre-existing VDR protein, allowing similar upstream signals to produce context-dependent transcriptional outcomes across different cellular environments.
   
4. Conclusion and Limitations
4.1 Conclusion
   This study provides a systems-level framework linking gene-level variability with pathway-level recurrent patterns across cellular contexts, rather than identifying isolated gene signatures.
   Vitamin D–related perturbations in the LINCS L1000 dataset induce structured but context-dependent transcriptional responses across diverse cellular models. While a limited subset of genes exhibits recurrent modulation across cell lines, the overall transcriptional landscape is dominated by context-dependent variation. At the pathway level, these responses show recurrent enrichment of shared functional programs related to metabolic regulation, stress response, and proliferative control, despite heterogeneity at the gene level. Notably, variability in transcriptional responses is not primarily explained by differential modulation of the VDR transcriptional axis, indicating that vitamin D signaling operates through activation of pre-existing receptor frameworks, with cellular context shaping downstream transcriptional outputs. Together, these findings are consistent with vitamin D responses being context-dependent adaptations that recurrently engage conserved biological processes, rather than a uniform activation of a canonical transcriptional program.
4.2 Limitations
   This study is based on LINCS L1000 transcriptional signatures, which combine direct measurement of landmark genes with computational inference of the remaining transcriptome. While this approach enables large-scale profiling and has been extensively validated, it may introduce uncertainty at the level of individual non-landmark genes. However, the transcriptional core identified here is supported by consistent signals across multiple cell lines and perturbational contexts, suggesting that it reflects robust biological patterns rather than algorithmic noise.
   The analysis was performed in cancer-derived cell lines (A549, HA1E, MCF7, PC3, and U2OS), which exhibit substantial genetic and epigenetic alterations compared to primary tissues. Accordingly, the contribution of cellular context to transcriptional variability may be amplified relative to physiological systems. However, variations seen in different models—including the unique patterns shown by MCF7 cells, probably due to their hormone-responsive nature—indicate that context-dependent responses reflect significant biological differences instead of being mere experimental artifacts.
   Perturbation doses in LINCS are designed to maximize transcriptional dynamic range and may exceed physiological concentrations for certain compounds, including vitamin D analogs. As such, the observed responses likely reflect upper-bound transcriptional effects and should be interpreted as perturbational trends rather than direct proxies of in vivo dose–response relationships.
   The proportion of variance explained by individual experimental factors was modest, which is expected in large-scale perturbational datasets characterized by substantial technical and biological variability. In this context, the relative contribution of factors—rather than their absolute explanatory power—is the most informative.
   As a sensitivity analysis, we repeated the consensus procedure using a stricter recurrence threshold (genes present in at least three cell lines instead of two). Under this criterion, the consensus set was reduced from 42/35 to 18/6 up- and downregulated genes, respectively. Notably, all genes identified under the stricter threshold (at least 3 votes) were fully contained within the original consensus (100% retention), indicating that the core transcriptional signal is robust to threshold selection and that additional genes identified under the more permissive criterion represent lower-consistency responses rather than distinct biological signals. Although the core_score is based on a consensus gene set defined by arbitrary thresholds, sensitivity analyses using alternative cutoffs yielded consistent results, supporting the overall robustness of the metric despite its threshold-based definition.
